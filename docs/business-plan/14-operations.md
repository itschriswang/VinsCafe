# Operations: how a Sunday actually runs

Six trading hours is the smallest part of the week. The work is on either side
of it. This is the run sheet for one service, written for the menu on the board
and for one person plus, at most, one helper.

## The constraint you keep hitting

Three things compete for the same pair of hands during service: **the machine**,
**the grill**, and **the counter**. One person can hold two of them. Nobody
holds three at a rush.

- The espresso machine is a 45-second cycle per drink and it never stops.
- The grill items — pho toastie, chilli prawn roll, kaya toast — are 4 to 7
  minutes each and they cannot be hurried or held.
- The counter is where the money is and where the queue is felt.

This is why the hot food is the throughput ceiling, not the coffee. Every
toastie ordered at 9:40am costs you roughly three coffees you did not make.
Price it, cap it, or batch it — but do not pretend it is free.

**Practical rule:** cap hot food at a fixed number of covers per hour and put
that number on the board. "Kitchen closes at 12" is a legitimate, common and
much-loved cafe policy. It also protects your one accessible service window.

## The week around the Sunday

| When | Work | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wednesday | Order beans, matcha, milk, eggs, prawns, dry goods. Count stock. | 1 |
| Thursday | Bake what freezes: cookie dough logged and frozen, tart shells blind-baked, basque batter portioned. | 3 |
| Friday | Deliveries in and temperature-checked on arrival. Log them. | 1 |
| Saturday | Bake fresh: croissants proved, cardamom buns, cake of the day. Prep kaya, chilli prawn mix, pho butter. Cold brew drawn (18h ahead). Clean down. Float counted. | 6 |
| **Sunday** | **Open 8–14. Prep from 6. Close-down to 16.** | **10** |
| Monday | Waste log, sales reconciliation, reorder, food safety records filed. | 1 |

That is roughly **22 hours a week for six hours of trade**. Any financial model
that values the owner's time at zero is telling you a story. The model in
[13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md) costs those hours explicitly, and you
should look at that line before anything else.

## Sunday run sheet

**06:00 — open up**
- Machine on, boiler to pressure (allow 30 min minimum; a cold group ruins the
  first ten shots). Grinder on, first dose to waste.
- Fridges: read and record temperatures. Anything above 5 °C is a decision, not
  a shrug — that is the record a council inspector reads first.
- Hand basin: warm water running, soap, paper towel. Check before anything else
  is touched. It is the single most-cited failure at inspection.
- Sanitiser made up fresh at the correct dilution. Date it.

**06:30 — bake and prep**
- Croissants and buns in. Cake of the day out to temper.
- Soft eggs for kaya toast: cook to order, never held. Eggs are the highest-risk
  item on this board and a held soft egg is the classic food-poisoning vector.
- Prawn mix out of the fridge in small batches only. Return the rest.
- Matcha sifted. Whisk, chawan and thermometer set. Water at 80 °C, not boiling.

**07:30 — dial in**
- Set the espresso recipe for the day and write it on the machine: dose, yield,
  time. Re-check at 10:00 as the hopper warms and the humidity moves.
- Milk jugs staged. Oat in reach — it is free on this menu, so it will be more
  than half of the milk poured.

**07:50 — front of house**
- Board up. Prices match `menu.json`; if they do not, the website is wrong and
  that is a consumer-law problem, not a cosmetic one.
- Allergen sheet at the counter, current, and everyone rostered has read it.
  Sesame, egg, dairy, gluten, crustacean and tree nut all appear on this board.
- Float in the till. EFTPOS tested with a $1 transaction, refunded.
- Queue line marked. On a court, the queue is the neighbours' problem before it
  is yours — keep it on your own land, off the road, off the nature strip.

**08:00 — open**

Order of service: take payment first, call the drink, fire the food. Never let
an unpaid ticket into the kitchen — at this scale you will lose it.

**Peak is 09:00–11:00.** Expect roughly half the day's transactions inside those
two hours. Everything you can pre-batch, pre-batch: cold brew poured to order
from a keg, lemonade and yuzu soda pre-mixed, cookies already on the plate.

**12:00 — kitchen closes.** Grill down and cleaned while drinks continue. This
is what makes a single-operator close possible at all.

**14:00 — close**
- Last coffee at 13:45. Say it out loud at 13:30.
- Waste log: what was made, what was sold, what was binned. This is the single
  most valuable number you will collect in year one — it tells you the bake
  quantities for next Sunday and it is most of the difference between a 32% and
  a 45% food cost.

**14:00–16:00 — close-down**
- Machine backflushed, group heads and baskets cleaned, grinder purged.
- Grill degreased. Grease arrestor checked.
- All surfaces cleaned and sanitised, floors last.
- Temperatures recorded again. Records filed.
- Cash counted, banked or secured, sales reconciled against the POS.
- Bins out to the **commercial** waste service, not the household kerbside bins.

## Records you must keep, and where

The food safety program is not paperwork for its own sake — it is the only
defence you have if someone gets sick. Keep, at minimum:

- **Temperature records**: fridges and freezer, twice per trading day; hot food
  probe checks; delivery temperatures on receipt.
- **Cleaning schedule**, signed off.
- **Supplier list** with contact details, so a recall can be traced in an hour.
- **Staff training records**, including the Food Safety Supervisor's certificate.
- **Waste and disposal log.**
- **Customer complaint log** — every complaint about illness, written down, with
  the date, what was eaten and what you did.

Keep them for the period your registration requires and keep them on paper as
well as in a phone. A dead phone on inspection day is not an excuse.

## Stock and ordering

At one day a week, your enemy is not running out — it is **freshness and
waste**. A 5 kg bag of beans that takes six weeks to get through is stale coffee
being sold at $4.00. Order small and often even when the price per kilo is worse.

- **Beans**: buy weekly, use within 3 weeks of roast. Ask your roaster for a
  weekly small drop rather than a monthly bulk one.
- **Matcha**: ceremonial grade degrades fast once opened and it is the most
  expensive input per gram on the board. Small tins, sealed, refrigerated,
  first-in-first-out, and never sifted more than a day ahead.
- **Milk and oat**: the one item where a Sunday-only cafe bleeds. You cannot
  buy Sunday's milk on Saturday and keep the remainder for six days. Order to a
  hard forecast and accept a small stockout risk.
- **Prawns**: frozen, cooked from frozen, never refrozen. Given the risk and the
  38% food cost, this is the first item to reconsider if margins bite.

## Single points of failure

At this size, everything is a single point of failure. Have an answer ready for:

- **The machine dies at 08:15.** Who services it, do they work Sundays, and what
  is the batch-brew fallback that keeps you selling something?
- **You are sick.** A one-person cafe with a fixed weekly slot has no depth. The
  honest answer is that you close, and you tell people early on the site.
- **Power cut.** Everything in the fridges is a judgement call. Write the rule
  now, cold: above 5 °C for more than 2 hours, it goes.
- **A complaint to council.** Have the file ready: permits, registration,
  insurance, food safety records. The difference between an inspection and an
  enforcement action is usually how organised you look in the first ten minutes.
