## The two-dollar test

Risks 1 and 2 are the only ones that are both **potentially fatal** and
**resolvable this week for under $100**. A title search and a phone call to your
insurer. Everything else in this plan is downstream of those two answers, which
is why [16-critical-path.md](16-critical-path.md) puts them first and puts
nothing before them.

## The risk nobody registers

Risk 11 — burnout — is the one that actually closes most small food businesses,
and it is the one least visible in a spreadsheet. Twenty-two hours a week for six
hours of trade, every week, for a result the model says is negative, is a hard
thing to sustain past the first year of novelty.

If the business is a **hobby that washes its face**, that is a legitimate and
honourable goal — but then the compliance capital in
[13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md) is wildly disproportionate to it, and
[11-alternatives.md](11-alternatives.md) is where to look.

If it is meant to be a **business**, it needs more trading days than a
residential garage on a cul-de-sac will ever be allowed to support.

That tension is the real finding of this whole plan, and no permit resolves it.
