# Insurance and liability

**Bottom line: running an undisclosed business from your home can void your home
and contents policy. Not reduce a claim — void the policy, including for things
that have nothing to do with the cafe. This is the cheapest catastrophic risk in
the whole project to eliminate, and the phone call that eliminates it is free.**

## The home policy problem

Two findings, both **[CONFIRMED]** across insurer and broker sources:

> Running a business from home **can void your residential building insurance**
> unless you notify your insurer or take out a commercial endorsement.

> Home and contents policies typically **cannot cover liability claims involving
> your trade or business**.

Put concretely. If a registered commercial kitchen is operating in your garage
and you have not told your insurer:

- A **grill fire** that takes the house is a business-use fire on a policy that
  excludes business use.
- A **customer slipping on the driveway** is a business liability claim on a
  policy whose liability section excludes business activity.
- And in the worst case, an **unrelated** claim — a burst pipe, a burglary — can
  be jeopardised too, because non-disclosure goes to the whole contract.

The house is very likely the largest asset involved. Risking it to avoid a
premium increase is a catastrophically bad trade.

### The disclosure duty

Since October 2021 the duty on consumer insurance is to **take reasonable care
not to make a misrepresentation**. **[LIKELY]** Answer the renewal questions
honestly, and tell them in writing about the change of use. Keep the reply.

**Do this before you spend anything on the fitout.** If the insurer says they
will not insure a dwelling with a commercial food premises attached — which is a
real possible answer — you need to know that at the $0 mark, not the $100,000
mark. It goes in [16-critical-path.md](16-critical-path.md) as one of the two
cheap kill-switches.

## What cover you actually need

Indicative annual premiums, **[VERIFY]** all of them — get a broker to quote the
real thing:

| Cover | Why | Indicative |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| **Public liability** ($10m–$20m) | Customers on your property. Usually required by council and any market or event you trade at. | $800–$1,500 |
| **Product liability** | Someone gets sick from your food. Usually bundled with public liability. | included |
| **Business contents / equipment** | The espresso machine alone is $6,000–$22,000. | $700–$1,200 |
| **Home policy endorsement or uplift** | Keeping the house insured at all. | $500–$1,000 |
| **Business interruption** | The machine dies, the council closes you, you cannot trade. | optional |
| **Food contamination / recall** | Contaminated stock, forced disposal. | optional |
| **Income protection** | You are the entire workforce. | strongly recommended |
| **Workers compensation** | Compulsory if you employ. See [07-employment.md](07-employment.md). | if employing |

The model in [13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md) carries $2,800 a year across
liability, contents and the home uplift. That is a placeholder; a broker will
sharpen it.

**Public liability is often a precondition** of council food registration, and
certainly of trading at any market or event. **[LIKELY]** So it is not optional
even in the minimal version of this business.

## The specific exposures here

Worth naming, because they drive both the cover and the operational controls in
[14-operations.md](14-operations.md):

- **Anaphylaxis.** The board carries sesame, egg, dairy, gluten, crustacean and
  probably tree nut. A mis-answered allergen question is the single
  highest-consequence event that can happen in this business. It is also the one
  most likely to occur on a busy Sunday when you are alone and rushing.
- **Food poisoning from eggs and prawns.** Soft eggs and prawns are the two
  highest-risk items on the menu, cooked by one person also running a coffee
  machine.
- **Scalding.** Hot drinks served to customers, including to children, in a
  domestic setting. A well-known and frequently litigated claim type.
- **The driveway.** Customers walking up a residential driveway while cars
  reverse out of it. Wet in winter, sloping, no line marking, no lighting
  designed for public use.
- **Children.** In a residential setting, unsupervised, near a road, near a grill.
- **Dogs.** Yours, or a customer's, on your property.

## Structure does not substitute for insurance

A company limits liability for the *business's* debts. It does not protect the
*house*, because the house is yours and the incident happened on your land. If
you are the person who made the coffee, you can be personally joined to a
negligence claim regardless of the entity.

For a business physically inside your home, insurance is the actual protection
and the entity choice is mostly a tax and administration question. See
[06-structure-tax-gst.md](06-structure-tax-gst.md).

## Australian Consumer Law

Consumer guarantees apply to the food and the retail goods: they must be of
acceptable quality and fit for purpose. **[LIKELY]** For a cafe this is mostly
uncontroversial, but two things matter:

- **Displayed prices must be honoured.** If `menu.json` and the board disagree,
  that is a compliance issue, not a cosmetic one — see
  [14-operations.md](14-operations.md).
- **Mandatory food recall** obligations apply if you sell packaged goods that
  turn out to be unsafe. Keep the supplier list current so you can trace a batch
  in an hour.

## Ask a broker who does hospitality

- I am converting my garage into a registered commercial food premises. Will my current home insurer continue to cover the dwelling, and at what premium?
- What public liability limit do I need for council registration and for markets?
- Does the policy cover allergen and food contamination claims?
- What happens to cover if I later trade from a van instead?
- What income protection is available to a sole trader with a single trading day?
