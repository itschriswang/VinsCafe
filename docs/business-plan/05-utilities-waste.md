# Trade waste, water, and getting the rubbish out

**Bottom line: none of this is a dealbreaker, but it is roughly $5,000 a year of
permanent cost and a grease arrestor buried in the yard, and almost nobody
budgets for any of it.**

## Trade waste — Yarra Valley Water

Vermont South sits in **Yarra Valley Water**'s area. **[LIKELY — confirm]**

A food business discharging kitchen wastewater to sewer needs a **trade waste
agreement**. The published costs:

| | Amount | |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Application fee | **$72.82** | one-off, to assess the business **[LIKELY]** |
| Contract fee | **$625.35 / year** | billed quarterly **[LIKELY]** |

Fees are risk-ranked, and most food businesses attract the lowest band.
**[LIKELY]**

**A residential property holding a commercial trade waste agreement is the part
to check.** It should be possible, but confirm it directly with Yarra Valley
Water before assuming — it is a five-minute phone call that de-risks a large
chunk of the fitout. **[VERIFY]**

## The grease arrestor

The majority of retail food businesses are **required to install a grease trap**
as a condition of the trade waste agreement. You cannot pay a fee instead — Yarra
Valley Water will levy additional treatment charges until one is installed, and
that is on top of, not in place of, the obligation. **[CONFIRMED]**

- **Installation**: budget **$4,000–$12,000** supplied and installed. **[VERIFY]**
  The variable is excavation and how far it is to the sewer connection.
- **Servicing**: **$150–$400 per quarter**, monthly or quarterly depending on
  volume. **[LIKELY]** Call it **$1,200 a year**.
- Failure to maintain it breaches the agreement and can lead to penalties or
  suspension of your discharge consent. **[CONFIRMED]**

Physically, it is a tank in the ground with a truck coming to pump it out on a
schedule. On a suburban block, think about where that tank goes and where the
truck parks — in a cul-de-sac, that is a neighbour conversation. See
[09-neighbours.md](09-neighbours.md).

## Backflow prevention

Commercial food premises generally require a **testable backflow prevention
device** on the water supply, with **mandatory annual testing** by a licensed
plumber and registration with the water authority. **[VERIFY]** Budget
**$1,200–$3,000** to install and **~$250 a year** to test.

## Plumbing

A licensed plumber, a **VBA plumbing permit** and a compliance certificate.
**[LIKELY]** The requirements that drive cost:

- **Warm water to the hand basin** — the Food Standards Code requires warm, not
  cold, which usually means a tempering valve and sometimes a dedicated unit.
- Enough hot water capacity for a commercial wash-up, which a domestic system
  generally does not have.
- Separate food prep sink, plus a double-bowl or dishwasher, plus the dedicated
  hand basin. Three distinct plumbing points minimum, before the coffee machine.
- A **mop sink / cleaner's sink**, which is easy to forget and is usually asked for.

The espresso machine also needs plumbed water with filtration, and a drain.

## Rates, tariffs and the quiet reclassification

Two recurring costs that catch people out. Both **[VERIFY]** — ask directly.

**Water and sewer tariffs.** A property with a commercial food premises may be
reclassified from residential to non-residential for water charging, which
changes the fixed charges as well as the usage rate.

**Council rates.** Whitehorse, like most councils, applies a different
rate-in-the-dollar to commercial property. If part of your home is reclassified
as commercial, the rates bill moves. This can be a meaningful recurring number
and it is worth asking the council's rates team explicitly, in writing, before
you commit.

## Commercial waste

**Council kerbside bins are for domestic waste.** A registered food business
generally needs its own commercial waste contract. **[LIKELY]** Putting cafe
waste in the household bins is both a breach and, in a court where everyone can
see your bins, an obvious one.

Budget **~$2,400 a year** for general waste, recycling and organics for an
operation this size. **[VERIFY]** Also arrange:

- **Used cooking oil** collection, if the grill produces any.
- **Coffee grounds** — heavy, wet, and a genuine volume problem. Most small
  cafes give them away to gardeners, which is free and good marketing.
- Cardboard, which is most of the volume from a bakery-heavy operation.

Under the *Environment Protection Act 2017* you carry a **general environmental
duty** and specific waste duties — including a duty to ensure waste goes to a
lawful place. **[LIKELY]**

## The annual total

Pulled from [13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md):

| | Annual |
| --- | ---: |
| Trade waste agreement | $625 |
| Grease arrestor servicing | $1,200 |
| Backflow annual test | $250 |
| Commercial waste collection | $2,400 |
| Utilities uplift | $3,000 |
| **Subtotal** | **$7,475** |

That is before insurance, registration, accounting or compliance statements.
It is also, notably, roughly a third of what the whole business earns in gross
profit in the lean scenario — which is the argument of
[01-the-verdict.md](01-the-verdict.md) in miniature.

## Ask

**Yarra Valley Water (1300 771 865):** Can a residential-titled property hold a
food business trade waste agreement? What grease arrestor size would this menu
require? What are the current application and contract fees? Is a backflow
device required?

**Whitehorse rates and Whitehorse waste services:** Does a registered food
premises at a residential address change my rates category? Can I keep using
kerbside bins, and if not, who do you recommend?
