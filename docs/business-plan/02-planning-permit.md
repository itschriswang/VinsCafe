# Planning permit and land use

**Bottom line: a cafe here is permit-required, not prohibited — but it is a
fully discretionary application in a residential cul-de-sac, advertised to the
neighbours, decided on exactly the grounds where a court is weakest.**

## First, find out what zone you are actually in

Everything below assumes **General Residential Zone (GRZ)**. It has not been
verified, and it is the most important unverified fact in this plan. **[VERIFY]**

Whitehorse applies both General Residential and **Neighbourhood Residential Zone
(NRZ)** across its suburbs, and NRZ is more restrictive. Check the zone for
10 Katupna Ct before anything else — it is free, it takes five minutes, and it
can change the answer.

- Whitehorse planning scheme maps, or VicPlan (`mapshare.vic.gov.au/vicplan`)
- Or ring Whitehorse statutory planning and ask

While you are there, check for overlays: **Neighbourhood Character**,
**Significant Landscape**, **Heritage**, **Design and Development**. Vermont
South has substantial post-1970s subdivision with character controls in places.
Any overlay adds another permit trigger and another set of objection grounds.

## The land use question

A cafe is a **food and drink premises** — a retail land use, not a residential
one. **[CONFIRMED]**

In the General Residential Zone, food and drink premises sit in **Section 2 —
permit required**. **[LIKELY]** They are *not* in Section 3, which would make
them prohibited outright. So the use is possible.

There is a Section 1 (no permit needed) pathway for food and drink premises in
residential zones, but it is conditioned on the land being **within 100 metres
of a Commercial or Mixed Use Zone, with the same street frontage, and adjoining
or having access to a road in a Road Zone**. **[LIKELY]**

Katupna Ct is a residential cul-de-sac. It meets none of those. So the Section 1
exemption is unavailable and you are in Section 2: **a discretionary permit
application**.

That is the whole game. Not a tick-box. A judgement call by a council planner,
with your neighbours invited to comment.

## Clause 52.11 Home Based Business, and why it does not rescue you

Clause 52.11 is the provision that lets people run businesses from home without
a planning permit. It is worth understanding precisely, because it is the thing
everyone assumes will cover a garage cafe, and it does not quite.

**No permit is required if all of these hold** **[CONFIRMED]**:

- The person conducting the business uses the dwelling as their **principal
  place of residence**.
- **No more than two people who do not live in the dwelling** work there at any
  one time.
- The net floor area used, **including storage**, does not exceed **100 m² or
  one-third of the net floor area of the dwelling, whichever is the lesser**.
- **No goods other than goods manufactured or serviced in the business may be
  offered for sale** — except goods offered for sale **online**.
- Only **one commercial vehicle** (≤2 tonnes, registered to a resident), not
  fuelled or repaired on site.
- No load on any utility greater than normal domestic use.
- **The business must not adversely affect the amenity of the neighbourhood** —
  including appearance, noise, traffic, hours, smell.

With a permit, those first two can stretch to **three non-resident staff** and
**200 m²**. **[LIKELY]**

### What this means for your board

The floor area is fine — a suburban double garage is roughly 35 m², well inside
the cap.

The **goods for sale** clause is the sharp edge. Read against
[`menu.json`](../../menu.json):

| Board section | Made on site? | Sellable under 52.11? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee, Matcha, Cold | Yes, made to order | **Yes** |
| Kitchen (toasties, prawn roll, kaya toast) | Yes, cooked on site | **Yes** |
| Desserts | Yes, if baked on site | **Yes** — bought-in cakes, no |
| **Take home**: beans 250g, matcha 30g, filter papers | **No — bought in and resold** | **No** (online only) |

So the *Take home* section cannot ride on the home-business exemption at all.
Filter papers are not even food. Either drop that section from the counter, sell
it through the website, or accept that it needs the permit.

### The real limit is amenity, not floor area

Note what Clause 52.11 does *not* say: it does not say customers cannot visit,
and it does not put a number on them. What it says is that the business **must
not adversely affect the amenity of the neighbourhood**.

A cafe drawing 48–132 people to a cul-de-sac on a Sunday morning, with a queue,
a grill exhaust and cars turning in a court, is going to be assessed against
that sentence. Reasonable people will disagree about the answer, which is
another way of saying: expect to need the permit, and expect it to be argued.

## Car parking — Clause 52.06

Two separate problems.

**You need to provide parking for the cafe.** Clause 52.06 sets rates for food
and drink premises; for restaurants it is commonly expressed per patron or per
100 m² of leasable floor area. **[VERIFY]** At any plausible rate, a 35 m² cafe
generates a requirement of a handful of spaces that a suburban block simply does
not have.

**You are also destroying parking.** Converting the garage removes the dwelling's
own required off-street space, which is itself a planning matter and, in some
schemes, its own permit trigger. **[VERIFY]**

The usual answer is to apply for a **reduction in the parking requirement** —
routine in an activity centre with public transport, much harder in a court
where the only place to put the overflow is the neighbours' frontages. Expect
this to be the central battleground of the application. See
[09-neighbours.md](09-neighbours.md).

## Signs — Clause 52.05

Residential zones are the most restrictive signage category in the scheme.
Business identification signage on a house is tightly limited and will generally
need its own permit, or be refused. **[LIKELY]**

Practically: assume **no sign**. This is less damaging than it sounds — the
business is a destination found through Instagram and the website, not passing
trade. But it does mean people will need the address, and that means publishing
your home address. See [12-concept-and-marketing.md](12-concept-and-marketing.md).

## The process, and what it costs you in time

1. **Pre-application meeting** with Whitehorse statutory planning. Free, and the
   highest-value hour in this entire project. Go before you spend anything.
2. **Lodge** the application with plans, a written submission addressing the
   decision guidelines, and usually a traffic and parking assessment.
3. **Fee** under the Planning and Environment (Fees) Regulations — a
   change-of-use application sits in the lower fee bands, but the real cost is
   the town planner you should engage to write it. Budget **$3,500–$12,000**
   all-in for planner, drawings and traffic advice. **[VERIFY]** on the
   statutory fee itself.
4. **Public notice.** Signs on site and letters to adjoining owners and
   occupiers. This is the moment the neighbours find out, and it is far better
   that they heard it from you first.
5. **Objections.** Anyone can object. Objections do not automatically defeat an
   application, but a volume of them typically pushes the decision from an
   officer under delegation to a council meeting, where it becomes political.
6. **Decision** — permit, notice of decision, or refusal. Statutory clock is 60
   days; advertised applications routinely take longer.
7. **VCAT** review if refused, or if a permit is granted and objectors appeal.
   Months, and legal cost. Budget $10,000+ if it gets there and you engage
   representation. **[VERIFY]**

## Realistically

A public cafe in a residential cul-de-sac garage, open one day a week, with no
off-street parking to spare, is a **hard application**. Not hopeless — the
one-day-a-week, low-intensity, no-signage framing is genuinely the best version
of the argument, and a council planner will recognise a modest proposal when
they see one. But you should assume:

- objections are likely,
- a parking reduction is the crux,
- conditions will cap hours, patron numbers and possibly seating,
- and refusal is a real outcome, at which point you have spent the money.

Which is why [11-alternatives.md](11-alternatives.md) exists, and why the
sequencing in [16-critical-path.md](16-critical-path.md) puts the free checks
first.

## Ask the council

- What zone and what overlays apply to 10 Katupna Ct?
- Is food and drink premises Section 2 in that zone here, and what conditions attach?
- What car parking rate applies, and how would a reduction be assessed in a court?
- Does converting the garage trigger a separate issue about the dwelling's own parking?
- Would you require a traffic report? A waste management plan? An acoustic report?
- What is the current application fee, and what is the realistic timeline for an advertised change of use?
