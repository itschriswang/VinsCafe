# Employment and the Sunday penalty rate

**Bottom line: one number decides the staffing model. A casual on a Sunday costs
about $54 an hour all-in, and on a $10.23 average transaction they must generate
nine extra sales an hour just to pay for themselves. In a six-hour service, that
is usually not possible. This is a one-person business.**

## Which award

A cafe is most likely covered by the **Restaurant Industry Award 2020
(MA000119)**. **[LIKELY]** The neighbouring possibilities are the Hospitality
Industry (General) Award (MA000009) and the Fast Food Industry Award (MA000003),
and the distinction turns on the nature of the service — table service versus
counter service versus fast food.

Get this confirmed. Applying the wrong award is one of the most common and most
expensive small-business mistakes, and the penalty rate structures differ.
Fair Work Ombudsman's Pay and Conditions Tool will resolve it, or ring them —
it is free.

## The number

Restaurant Industry Award, **casual Level 2 (Food and Beverage Attendant Grade
2)** — the classification a competent barista or all-rounder sits at:

| | Rate |
| --- | ---: |
| Ordinary hourly (full-time / part-time) | ~$27.08 **[LIKELY]** |
| Casual ordinary (incl. 25% loading) | ~$33.85 **[LIKELY]** |
| **Casual Sunday (150%, incl. casual loading)** | **$48.46** **[LIKELY]** |
| Plus superannuation at 12% | **≈ $54.28 all-in** |

**[VERIFY] all of these against the Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide before you pay
anyone.** Award rates change every 1 July and these are search-derived, not
taken from the pay guide itself.

Public holidays are higher again. Sunday is the single most expensive ordinary
trading day in Australian hospitality, and it is the only day you trade.

## What that does to the business

From [13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md), with one helper for the six-hour
service across 48 Sundays:

| | Lean (no staff) | Base (1 helper) | Stretch (2+ helpers) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Wages | $0 | −$15,633 | −$36,476 |
| EBITDA | −$5,237 | −$10,117 | −$16,623 |

Hiring one person makes the annual result **$4,880 worse**, and hiring properly
for the stretch scenario makes it **$11,386 worse** than doing it alone.

The break-even arithmetic is brutal and worth internalising: at a **$6.22
contribution per transaction**, one hour of casual Sunday labour at $54.28 needs
**8.7 additional transactions** to pay for itself. Not eight and a half
transactions served — eight and a half *additional* ones that would not have
happened without that person. In a 14-transactions-per-hour service, that is more
than half the entire hour's trade.

**So: the base case is one person, and that person is you.** Everything in
[14-operations.md](14-operations.md) is written on that assumption.

## Minimum engagement

The award sets a **minimum engagement period** for casuals — commonly two or
three hours. **[VERIFY]** For a six-hour Sunday this is not a binding constraint,
but it matters if you ever think about bringing someone in just for the 9–11am
peak. You cannot roster someone for ninety minutes.

## Superannuation

The super guarantee is **12%** and the old $450-a-month threshold has been
**removed** — super is payable from the first dollar. **[LIKELY — VERIFY the
current rate and the payment timing rules, including whether payday super
obligations apply]**

This applies to casuals. Budget it as part of the hourly cost, which is why the
all-in figure above is $54.28 rather than $48.46.

## Workers compensation

**WorkSafe Victoria** registration becomes compulsory once annual remuneration
exceeds a threshold — commonly cited at **$7,500**. **[VERIFY]** The base
scenario's $15,633 wage bill clears it comfortably, so if you employ anyone at
all, assume you must register.

A **sole trader is not covered by their own workers compensation policy**.
**[LIKELY]** If you injure yourself — a burn, a slip, a back — there is no
workers comp behind you. Personal income protection insurance is the substitute,
and for a one-person business whose entire revenue depends on that person being
able to stand up on a Sunday, it is close to essential. See
[08-insurance-and-liability.md](08-insurance-and-liability.md).

## Family help

The instinct in a one-day-a-week family cafe is that a partner, a sibling or a
teenager helps out for nothing. Be careful.

- If someone is genuinely a **business partner** or has an ownership interest,
  they are not an employee.
- If they are **directed to perform work** in the way an employee would be, they
  are an employee, and the award, super and workers comp all apply — regardless
  of what everyone agreed informally. **[LIKELY]**
- **"Volunteers" in a for-profit business are not a real category.** A commercial
  cafe cannot take volunteer labour.

Unpaid family labour is extremely common and it is also one of the things that
turns into a large, retrospective, interest-bearing problem if a relationship
sours. Underpayment enforcement has been strengthened in recent years, including
criminal provisions for deliberate underpayment. **[VERIFY]** the current
position.

If family will regularly work the service, the clean answer is to employ them
properly and put the cost in the model.

**Juniors** attract percentage-of-adult rates by age and are the cheapest lawful
labour. **[LIKELY]** A 17-year-old on a Sunday is a materially different cost
from an adult casual, and for counter and clearing work that is a legitimate
option.

## Occupational health and safety

The moment anyone works there, **your home is a workplace** under the
*Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004* (Vic), and you owe duties both to
employees and to visitors. **[LIKELY]**

The real hazards in this operation:

- **Burns** — steam wands, the grill, 80 °C matcha water, hot oil.
- **Slips** — wet floors behind a machine, and a residential driveway in the rain.
- **Manual handling** — milk crates, 5 kg bean bags, the grease arrestor.
- **Cuts** — mandolines, bread knives.
- **Customers and children on the property**, including in a driveway where cars
  reverse. This is the one that keeps people awake.

Have a first aid kit, a fire blanket and extinguisher, and written safe work
instructions for the machine and the grill. Not because a regulator will ask,
but because a one-person business has no cover when the one person is hurt.

## Records

If you employ anyone: payslips within one working day of payment, time and wage
records kept seven years, super paid on time and recorded, and Single Touch
Payroll reporting each pay run. **[LIKELY]** Cheap payroll software handles all
of it and costs less than one hour of Sunday labour a month.
