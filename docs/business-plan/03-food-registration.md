# Food Act registration and food safety

**Bottom line: this board is a Class 2 food business, and Victorian councils
consistently take the position that Class 2 cannot be run from a domestic
kitchen. The garage has to become a real commercial kitchen, or the menu has to
change.**

## The class system

Victoria classifies food businesses 1 to 4 by risk under the *Food Act 1984*.
**[CONFIRMED]**

| Class | What it covers | Program | Supervisor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hospitals, aged care, childcare — vulnerable people | Independent FSP | Yes |
| **2** | **Unpackaged potentially hazardous food** — cooked, ready to eat | **Food Safety Program required** | **Yes** |
| 3 | Low-risk, or pre-packaged potentially hazardous food | No FSP; basic records only | No |
| 3A / 4 | Lowest risk — sealed shelf-stable goods, drinks | Minimal | No |

### Which one is your board?

**Class 2.** Not arguable. **[CONFIRMED]** The triggers, from
[`menu.json`](../../menu.json):

- **Kaya toast, soft egg** — a soft-cooked egg is one of the highest-risk items
  in food service.
- **Singaporean chilli prawn roll** — crustacean, ready to eat.
- **Pho toastie** — hot cooked, unpackaged, ready to eat.
- Dairy in every latte, matcha latte and cortado.
- Cream/dairy desserts: basque cheesecake, custard tart.

Any one of those makes it Class 2. You have five.

## The finding that matters most

> Class 2 food businesses are **unlikely to meet the requirements of the Food
> Standards Code in a domestic kitchen**. Domestic kitchens are generally
> suitable only for small-scale, low-to-medium-risk activity — Class 3 and 3A.
> Large-scale and high-risk activity should be conducted from a **commercial
> kitchen**. **[CONFIRMED — consistent across multiple Victorian councils]**

Some councils will approve a domestic kitchen for a home food business "subject
to conditions", but that latitude is for Class 3, not for a public cafe cooking
eggs and prawns to order.

So there is no shortcut. The garage must be built as a commercial kitchen, which
is the same conclusion [04-building-and-access.md](04-building-and-access.md)
reaches from the building-code direction. The two requirements converge on the
same expensive answer.

**The corollary is the opportunity.** Drop the eggs, the prawns and the
unpackaged hot food, and you are a **Class 3** business: no Food Safety Program,
no Food Safety Supervisor, and a domestic kitchen becomes plausible. Serving
**coffee and tea with milk for immediate consumption is expressly Class 3**.
**[CONFIRMED]** That is the entire basis of the recommendation in
[11-alternatives.md](11-alternatives.md).

## What Class 2 registration actually requires

**Register with the City of Whitehorse** as the registering council, before you
trade. Operating unregistered is an offence under the Food Act. **[CONFIRMED]**

The sequence, and the order matters:

1. **Submit plans before you build.** Councils assess food premises plans at
   design stage. Building the fitout first and asking afterwards is how people
   end up demolishing new joinery. This step is free and it is the one people
   skip.
2. **Application and registration fee**, annually renewable. Whitehorse's
   current Class 2 fee could not be sourced here — budget **$700–$900** as a
   placeholder and confirm. **[VERIFY]**
3. **Pre-registration inspection** by an Environmental Health Officer.
4. **Food Safety Program (FSP).** Class 2 must have one — either the Department
   of Health **standard template** (free, and adequate for a business this
   simple) or an independently written non-standard program. **[CONFIRMED]**
5. **Food Safety Supervisor (FSS).** A nominated person with the required
   competencies, reasonably available to supervise. For a one-person cafe that
   is you. Training is a short accredited course, typically **$150–$300**.
   **[VERIFY]** on current cost.
6. **Ongoing**: annual renewal, unannounced inspections, and the records.

## The physical requirements — Food Standards Code 3.2.2 and 3.2.3

This is what turns a garage into a kitchen, and what the capital estimate in
[13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md) is mostly paying for. **[LIKELY]** on
specifics; confirm each with the EHO at plan stage.

- **A dedicated hand washing basin**, used for nothing else, with **warm running
  water**, soap and single-use towel, positioned where food handlers actually
  work. The most commonly cited failure at inspection.
- **A separate food preparation sink**, and a **double-bowl sink or commercial
  dishwasher** for washing and sanitising. The hand basin cannot be any of these.
- **Smooth, impervious, cleanable finishes** to floors, walls and ceilings —
  sealed commercial-grade flooring with coved junctions, washable wall linings.
  A plasterboard-and-carpet garage fails on every surface.
- **Mechanical ventilation** over the grill, discharging where it does not
  become the neighbours' problem. See [09-neighbours.md](09-neighbours.md).
- **Adequate lighting**, **pest proofing** (sealed gaps, screened openings — a
  roller door is a pest-proofing problem), and **rubbish and recycling storage**
  that does not attract vermin.
- **Toilets must not open directly into a food handling area** — relevant if the
  plan is to route staff or patrons through the house.
- **Temperature control**: refrigeration holding ≤5 °C, hot holding ≥60 °C,
  calibrated probe thermometer, and the records to prove it.
- **Adequate potable water supply** and proper sewage disposal — see
  [05-utilities-waste.md](05-utilities-waste.md).

## Allergens

The board carries **sesame** (black sesame cookie), **egg**, **dairy**,
**gluten**, **crustacean** (prawn) and likely **tree nut**. Under the Plain
English Allergen Labelling requirements, allergens must be declared using
specific plain-English terms, and for unpackaged food sold at a counter you must
be able to answer allergen questions accurately on request. **[LIKELY]**

Practically: a written allergen matrix at the counter, kept current, and everyone
who serves has read it. This is also the highest-consequence risk on the whole
board — see [15-risk-register.md](15-risk-register.md).

## Labelling the retail items

The 250g beans and 30g matcha sold to take home are packaged food and need
compliant labels: name, ingredients, allergens, net weight, date marking, lot
identification, and supplier name and address. **[LIKELY]** If you buy them in
and resell them unopened in the supplier's packaging, that is the supplier's
label doing the work — but note from [02-planning-permit.md](02-planning-permit.md)
that reselling bought-in goods is exactly what Clause 52.11 does not allow.
