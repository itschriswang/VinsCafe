# Building code, change of use and access

**Bottom line: a garage is Class 10a, a cafe is Class 6, and moving between them
is a building permit, an occupancy permit and a full compliance upgrade. But
there is one rule worth designing the whole business around: cap the room at 20
people and the most expensive line in the fitout disappears.**

## The classification change

Under the National Construction Code:

- A domestic garage is **Class 10a** — a non-habitable appurtenant structure.
- A cafe is **Class 6** — "eating rooms, cafes, restaurants… used for the supply
  of services direct to the public". **[CONFIRMED]**

Going from 10a to 6 is a **change of use**, which under the *Building Act 1993*
and the *Building Regulations 2018* requires a **building permit**, and because
the new use is a public one, an **occupancy permit**. **[LIKELY]**

You will need a **registered building surveyor** — private or municipal — to
issue both. Engage one early, before the architect and well before the builder;
they will tell you what the building must become, and that determines the budget.

## The 20-person rule — read this before designing anything

Under **NCC Part F4**, patron sanitary facilities **need not be provided where
the total number of persons accommodated in the building is not more than 20**.
**[LIKELY — confirm with your building surveyor]**

Unisex accessible sanitary compartments are required **on each storey where
sanitary compartments are provided**. Provide none for patrons, and there is
nothing that has to be made accessible.

This is worth **$12,000 to $30,000** — the single largest line in the fitout, and
the one that most often makes small conversions impossible. A 35 m² garage
seating fifteen with a takeaway window is a completely different project from one
seating thirty.

**Design for twenty. Put it in the planning application as a condition you are
volunteering.** It helps the planning argument too — a self-imposed patron cap
is exactly the kind of amenity control a council likes to see offered rather
than imposed. See [02-planning-permit.md](02-planning-permit.md).

Staff facilities are a separate question and you still need them, but the house
can generally serve that — subject to the Food Standards Code rule that a toilet
must not open directly into a food handling area. See
[03-food-registration.md](03-food-registration.md).

## What else a Class 6 conversion drags in

All **[LIKELY]** — your building surveyor's scope, and the reason to get one
early.

**Fire separation from the dwelling.** This is the big structural one. A Class 6
part attached to a Class 1a house generally requires fire-resisting construction
between them — rated walls, rated and self-closing doors. An internal door from
the garage into the house is the obvious problem.

**Egress.** Number of exits, travel distances, door swing direction and hardware
that opens without a key from inside. A roller door is not an exit.

**Emergency lighting and exit signs** (NCC Part E4), which may or may not be
triggered at this floor area — surveyor's call.

**Accessibility.** Even with no accessible toilet, the *Disability Discrimination
Act 1992* applies to a new public premises independently of the NCC. Getting a
wheelchair from the street, up a residential driveway, over the garage threshold
and to the counter is a real design problem on a sloping suburban block. Budget
for a compliant path of travel, threshold and door width. **Do not treat the DDA
as optional because the NCC gave you a concession** — they are different
instruments, and a complaint under the DDA does not care what the surveyor
signed off.

**Mechanical ventilation.** A grill needs a proper kitchen exhaust hood to
AS 1668, with make-up air, and a discharge point that does not vent into a
neighbour's window. See [09-neighbours.md](09-neighbours.md).

**The fabric.** Insulation, lining, a sealed commercial-grade floor with slip
resistance, ceiling height, glazing, and weatherproofing the roller door opening
— which is also the pest-proofing problem the health officer will raise.

**Essential Safety Measures.** A Class 6 building carries ongoing ESM obligations
and an **annual essential safety measures statement**, with the maintenance
behind it. This is a permanent yearly cost, not a one-off — it is in the fixed
costs in [13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md).

## What it costs

From [13-the-numbers.md](13-the-numbers.md), the compliance-driven portion of
the capital, low to high:

| Line | Low | High |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Building permit, surveyor, drawings | $4,000 | $14,000 |
| Structural works, lining, insulation, floor | $8,000 | $30,000 |
| Fire separation from the dwelling | $3,000 | $12,000 |
| Accessible patron toilet — **avoidable at ≤20 persons** | **$0** | $30,000 |
| Accessible entry, ramp, path of travel | $3,000 | $15,000 |
| Mechanical exhaust hood and make-up air | $6,000 | $18,000 |
| Plumbing: sinks, hand basin, warm water | $5,000 | $14,000 |
| Electrical, possible three-phase | $3,000 | $15,000 |

These are **[VERIFY]** ranges for Melbourne 2026, not quotes. The spread is
genuinely that wide, and where you land depends almost entirely on the condition
of the existing slab, the roof, and how far the nearest drain is.

## The espresso machine and the power supply

A two-group commercial espresso machine typically needs either a dedicated
high-amperage circuit or three-phase supply. **[VERIFY]** Upgrading a suburban
house's supply is a distributor job, and it is one of the classic budget
surprises in small fitouts. Ask the electrician before you buy the machine, not
after — and ask the machine supplier for the exact electrical requirement in
writing.

## Ask your building surveyor

- Confirm the classification and that an occupancy permit is required.
- At what patron number do patron sanitary facilities become mandatory here? Can we design to stay under it?
- What fire separation is required between the Class 6 part and the dwelling, and what happens to the internal connecting door?
- Do emergency lighting and exit signs apply at this floor area?
- What is required for a compliant accessible path of travel from the street, and what is our DDA exposure if we provide no accessible toilet?
- What are the Essential Safety Measures for this building, and what will the annual statement cost to maintain?
- Does the loss of the garage create a problem with the dwelling's own required parking?
